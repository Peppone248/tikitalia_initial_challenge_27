How the HMM decides who has the ball

The model with the highest spellF1 on the validation matches decides two things in a single pass: who has possession and when play is stopped. This page shows what it reads, how it recognises each state and why it does not change its mind at every frame.

H possession of the home team A possession of the away team D dead ball: play stopped or ball out

Read this first: what this page is not

This is the model of the morning of 04/11. The current pipeline is combined_algorithm (combined_algorithm/REPORT_COMBINED.md): it keeps the single H/A/D HMM, but step 1 becomes our own tracking and steps 3a and 3b become gradient-boosted trees with new inputs. It is scored on a different benchmark, so its numbers cannot be compared with the ones below. That report also finds that the degraded scenario used here is clearly not like the provided match.

Which model is mapped

The single H/A/D HMM (REPORT_B, section 17). On IDSSE it has the highest spellF1: 0.5895 with degraded tracking and 0.7137 with clean tracking. Inside it uses the "team model" of section 15, which remains the best variant on the 5-minute public window (spellF1 0.6815 and macroF1 0.8889). That window has 17 possessions and noise of about ±0.39, so it cannot be used to choose.

The report does not give the macroF1 of the HMM on IDSSE. The only value available is the one of the public window: 0.8521.

The map

Step by step

  1. 0

    What it receives

    The model sees no video and no audio. It works on three sources:

    • ATD tracking: the position of 22 players and the ball, 25 frames per second. The ball is missing on 40.7% of the graded frames of BAR-ATH, often exactly in confused play (crowded areas, aerial duels).
    • Smart Tagging: automatic labels of throw-ins, corners, goal kicks, free kicks and kick-offs. They are not the truth and contain errors. They cover almost all corners and kick-offs, about 6 throw-ins out of 10 and few free kicks (no tag for fouls and offsides).
    • ATD metadata: the role of each player, used to recognise the goalkeepers.

    Only in training do the DFL labels of the IDSSE matches enter: BallPossession says who has the ball, BallStatus says whether the ball is live or dead.

  2. 1

    Tracking cleaning

    First, tracker errors with a physical or Laws-of-the-Game cause are corrected:

    • A jump of over 2.5 m between two frames equals 62 m/s: it is not a person, it is another track.
    • A goalkeeper who appears in the opposite box on a track that did not arrive there is actually the other goalkeeper, and the track moves to the other team.
    • R5: a fragment that restarts where an opposing track ended, while its team already has two extra players on the pitch, belongs to the opponent.
  3. 2

    Dead-ball rules

    Each rule produces a layer of frames that are candidates for D:

    • D-SLOW: the mean speed of the players stays under 1.1 m/s for at least 3 s and the ball is invisible for half of the stretch. It is for fouls and offsides, the hardest to see.
    • Ball at rest on the pitch for at least 2 s, until the kick; wall: at least 3 players of the same team close together and still.
    • Ball leaving the pitch (Law 9): D starts when the ball has crossed the line, not before.
    • R1 closes the stoppage at the first set-piece kick; R4 moves D-SLOW back to the point where the ball disappeared; R6 joins islands of play under 10 s between two stoppages; R3c recognises a ball lost while going out and found again on the same line.
    • c_v3: an earlier HMM for the dead ball, anchored on the Smart Tagging tags.
  4. 3

    Two learned classifiers

    A logistic regression adds up the features with weights and passes the result through a sigmoid, which gives a probability between 0 and 1. The weights are estimated with Newton steps and an L2 penalty that keeps them small. Training is deterministic: same data, same weights.

    3a · Who has the ball (49 features)

    • Control votes: a player votes if within 1.5 m of the ball and the ball moves under 8 m/s. Votes are summed on windows of 0.5, 1, 2 and 4 s, before and after the frame. The last and the next vote weigh less as they get further away in time, so a vote still counts when the ball disappears.
    • Closeness to the ball on three scales (1, 3 and 8 m) and ball position along the attacking direction.
    • Collective shape (home minus away): spread, length, width, forward speed, centroid, defensive line, most advanced player, mean speed. The team with the ball spreads out and moves up, the defending team gets compact. It works even without the ball.
    • Transitions: the mean of those measures in the second (or two) after the frame minus the mean before. At a change of possession the two teams swap roles, and it shows in the players.
    • Contacts and duels (Vidal-Codina et al., 2022): contact = ball close to a player who changes direction by over 30° or speed by over 2 m/s; duel = two opponents within 1 m of the ball.
    • Rule signals: goalkeeper catch and team taking the restart.

    All features change sign if H and A are swapped, and the model has no intercept. So with the teams swapped P(H) = 1 − P(H): it cannot learn "home has the ball more", it learns only the evidence. The largest weights are centroid (+4.6) and defensive line (−4.7), then the transitions, then the last and next vote.

    3b · Ball live or dead (40 inputs)

    It reads the final mask of the rules (also on windows of ±1, 3 and 8 s), the D of c_v3 and each rule layer. It adds the ball (seen or not, speed, how long since seen, last time seen outside the lines, distance from the line), the mean speed of the players on windows of 2 and 5 s, and the tag windows with the time since the kick. On per-frame live/dead accuracy it reaches 0.942 against 0.914 of the rules alone (degraded tracking) and 0.981 against 0.971 (clean).

  5. 4

    Evidence per frame

    The two probabilities give P(H), P(A) and P(D) for every frame. For each state the HMM uses w · (log P(class | frame) − log prior). The prior (the frequency of the class) is subtracted, so that a frequent class does not win only for that reason. w is the weight of the evidence: it is 1.0, and between 0.75 and 1.0 the result does not change by more than 0.002.

  6. 5

    The HMM decides the sequence

    A Hidden Markov Model assumes that the states H, A, D are hidden and that each one "produces" the data we observe. It looks for the most probable sequence of states over all the time, taking three things into account: the evidence of each frame, how long a state lasts at least, how likely it is to go from one state to another. How it recognises and keeps the states

How D is recognised

Model 3b decides it, frame by frame, reading the rule layers as clues. Then the HMM prevents stoppages under 2 s, which would split a possession of the same team in two. With the rules as the only source of D the HMM drops to spellF1 0.563 (degraded) and 0.696 (clean), so learned D matters.

How H and A are recognised

Model 3a decides it on live frames. A team weighs on a frame if it has just controlled the ball, if its collective shape is that of the attacking team, or if in the last second the two teams swapped roles. A real change needs several pieces of evidence in a row: in the two-state model of section 16, flipping took about 7 frames (0.3 s) with P = 0.8 against, or about 1 s with P = 0.6.

What it does when the ball is not seen

It is the most frequent case (40.7% of the frames) and there are four mechanisms. Control votes decay over time instead of disappearing suddenly. The collective shape does not depend on the ball. Model 3b has as inputs how long the ball has been unseen and where it was last seen. The HMM, with durations and transitions, covers the short gaps. Filling the gaps with a straight line did not help (at most +0.005): 75% of the frames without a ball in BAR-ATH lie in gaps longer than 15 s.

Limits to keep in mind

Domain gap. On the public window the F1 of D is 0.750 against 0.822 of the rules, and on the whole of BAR-ATH the HMM writes less D (39.9% against 43.6%). The classifier learned from IDSSE degraded with a simulation, and the real tracker of BAR-ATH loses the ball in a different way. With 17 possessions it is a warning, not a verdict.

Too few possessions. On BAR-ATH the HMM produces 301 possessions against the 317 of the rules. The largest margin, according to the oracle of section 20, lies in the changes the model does not find, not in their date.

State of the work. At this snapshot the HMM is not the default CSV (hmm3.enabled is false) and sections 19-21 of the report are provisional.

Glossary

ATD
The automatic tracking file supplied by the challenge: position of players and ball for every frame.
BAR-ATH
The match of the challenge (Barcelona-Athletic). It has public labels only for the first 5 minutes.
Bakis
"Left to right" topology: you can stay or go forward, never back. Here it imposes the minimum duration of a state.
DFL
Deutsche Fußball Liga. Its labels (BallPossession, BallStatus) come with the IDSSE matches.
degraded / clean
IDSSE degraded to resemble the tracker of BAR-ATH (ball gaps, broken tracks) and original IDSSE. "ts" adds simulated team swaps.
Emission
The evidence that a state produces what we observe in a frame. Here it comes from the two logistic regressions.
Feature
A number computed from the data and fed to the model (for example the centroid of the team).
Forward-backward (mpm)
Computes for each frame the probability of each state looking at past and future, and picks the most probable. With the best weights it gives the same result as Viterbi.
HMM
Hidden Markov Model: sequence model with hidden states, probabilities of going from one to another and probabilities that each state produces what is observed.
IDSSE
The 7 Bundesliga matches with DFL labels used to train and validate. They are other matches, so allowed by the rules.
IoU
Intersection over Union: how much a predicted possession and a true one overlap in time (shared duration divided by total duration).
L2
Penalty on large weights, which makes training stable. It equals a Gaussian prior on the weights.
Latency
By how many frames, on average, the predicted change comes before or after the true one.
Law 9
Law of the Game on the ball in and out of play: the ball is out only when it has wholly crossed the line.
LOMO
Leave-one-match-out: train on six matches and score on the seventh, in turn.
macroF1
Unweighted mean of the F1 of H, A and D, frame by frame. It measures whether the label is right on the right frame.
MAP
Maximum a posteriori: an estimate that uses a prior. The pseudo-count 1 (Dirichlet) avoids transitions with zero probability.
Prior
How often a class appears in general. It is subtracted (in log) so as not to favour the most common class.
Logistic regression
Weighted sum of the features passed through a sigmoid: it returns a probability.
Smart Tagging
Automatic tags of set pieces, in intervals (phases) of about 35 s that start a few seconds before the restart.
spellF1
The ranking metric. A possession is a continuous run of H or A. Predicted possessions are matched one to one with true ones by IoU and the F1 is averaged over thresholds 0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.6 and 0.7. Each possession counts once.
transF1@25
F1 on state changes, with a tolerance of ±25 frames (about 1 s).
Viterbi
Algorithm that finds the sequence of states with the highest total probability.
X
Abstention allowed in the file. For spellF1 it equals repeating the last label given, for macroF1 it is an error.
